The OSI Model and Its Seven Layers

The OSI model describes how data moves through a network in seven layers. Starting from the top they are: Application, Presentation, Session, Transport, Network, Data Link, and Physical. Each layer has its own job, and each one serves the layer above it while using the services of the layer below.

When you send a message, the data travels down the layers on your computer, crosses the network, and then climbs back up the layers on the receiving computer. This layer by layer design makes troubleshooting easier, because an engineer can isolate a problem to one layer, such as a broken cable at the physical layer or a wrong address at the network layer.
