TCP/IP: The Rules of the Internet

TCP/IP is the set of rules the internet runs on. It has four layers: Application, Transport, Internet, and Network Access. TCP, the Transmission Control Protocol, breaks your data into small packets, numbers them, and makes sure every packet arrives safely at the other end. IP, the Internet Protocol, gives each packet an address so it can find its way across many different networks.

TCP is reliable but slower, because it checks and resends anything that is lost. Its partner UDP is faster but does not check for errors, which is why live video and online games often use UDP, while web pages and downloads use TCP. Every internet service, from email to streaming, is built on this foundation.
